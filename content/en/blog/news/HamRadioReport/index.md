## COHIRADIA Sparked Interest at Ham-Radio 2026

The presentation of COHIRADIA at the GFGF booth at [Ham-Radio](https://www.hamradio-friedrichshafen.de/) in Friedrichshafen (June 26–28) was very successful. Numerous interested visitors learned about the background and technical design of COHIRADIA, as well as its various implementation platforms. The well-structured new website proved to be very helpful for quickly navigating to the relevant information. 

The entire platform, in all currently supported variants, was demonstrated interactively in live operation, and the setup invited many visitors to actively “tune through” the archive’s synthetic and real AM spectra. Thanks to Bernhard Nagel for providing his Philetta battery-powered receiver, which performed flawlessly in continuous operation as a magnetically coupled playback device. In addition, the signals were also fed via a wired connection to an Eumigette W and via a loop to a Minerva portable radio from the 1960s. I was able to demonstrate the COHIRADIA concept particularly vividly on June 27 with a longwave/mediumwave broadband recording that was only a few hours old, which I had successfully captured overnight on a field with low QRM outside Friedrichshafen using my mobile SDR station: It contains the last two hours of the long-wave station in Droitwich (BBC4) on 198 kHz, which went off the air at 2:00 a.m. on June 27.
We would also like to extend our warmest thanks to the GFGF team (Dirk Becker, Christoph Heiner, Bernhard Nagel, Rüdiger Walz) for providing the space, their very pleasant cooperation, and many constructive discussions. Thanks also to Claus-Peter Gallenmiller, who spent a day in Hall A and took over for me at the booth on several occasions. We were delighted to welcome Ueli Kurmann, the driving force behind the IT aspects of our project.

The following images illustrate the diversity of the playback systems on display; in addition to the COHIRADIA platform, visitors could also admire the concert transmitter paired with a beautiful Vitus by Rüdiger Walz (Image 6) and Dirk Becker’s demonstration of spectra synthesized via GNU Radio and played back on a portable radio (Image 7).
